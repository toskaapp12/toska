what toska is

toska is an anonymous space for heartbreak — a quiet app where people going through breakups write the things they never got to say, and read the words of others who are in it too.

there are no real names, no profile photos, and no follower counts to perform for. posts are short written reflections — grief, anger, longing, relief — shared under an anonymous handle with the people who understand them best: strangers going through the same thing.

the name comes from the russian word тоска — a dull ache of the soul, a longing with nothing to long for.

how it works

  • write it down. short posts, tagged by feeling — longing, anger, regret, still love you, moving on. a daily prompt asks the question you've been avoiding.
  • letters. longer-form mode for the message you'll never send — up to 2,000 characters of everything they never heard.
  • whispers & midnight posts. words that quietly disappear — after an hour, or at midnight — for the thoughts you need to say out loud but not keep.
  • feel it, don't perform it. readers can mark that they felt a post. no clout, no virality mechanics, no follower leaderboards.
  • share a card. turn a post into a quiet, beautiful image — with your anonymity intact.

safety & care

heartbreak is a vulnerable place, so toska is built defensively: every post and reply passes through layered content moderation for harassment, slurs, and personal information, on the device and again on the server.

when someone's words sound like more than heartbreak, the app responds gently — a private check-in with crisis resources (call or text 988, or text HOME to 741741 in the US) shown before anything is shared. content that signals real crisis is reviewed by a human.

we collect as little as possible, sell nothing, and export or delete everything on request. the details live in our privacy policy and terms of service.
